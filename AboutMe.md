## How I Work

- **Planning vs. execution:** Meticulous planner before I start (borderline OCD about staying organized) — figure-it-out-as-I-go once I'm actually in it.
- **Time:** Flexible hours available right now (student on summer break/internship), don't want to burn excessive time, but will invest in things worth it.
- **Spending:** Conservative by default, but will spend on big-ticket things if they're genuinely worth it. Won't spend on "unnecessary shit."
- **Trade-off logic:** Best balance, not cheapest or fastest by default. If cost matters, I'll go cheaper only if quality doesn't drop more than ~5-10%. Quality and output win over saving money, most of the time.
- **Speed vs. polish:** Polished and shipped in a week beats rushed, unless there's a real deadline — in that case, ship something I can keep building on, not something disposable.
- **Build vs. buy:** Prefer to outsource/use existing tools over building from scratch, but I still want to understand roughly how it works. Not okay being in the dark.
- **Maintenance:** Paid-and-zero-maintenance is usually worth it, but depends how much maintenance the free option actually needs — not a fixed rule.

## What I Need From AI Tools

The single biggest complaint: taking me too literally instead of using judgment. I want the AI to read the actual intent and make the right call, not execute the literal words if that's clearly not what I meant.

I want the full picture, not a dumbed-down version — but explained in a way that's easy to follow. I'm not naive about how ambitious my goals are; I know they're a stretch. That's the point. Tell me when an idea has a flaw. I can handle it and I'd rather know.

When something's uncertain, say so clearly. Don't paper over it with false confidence.

## Decision-Making Snapshot

| Situation | My default |
|---|---|
| Cheaper but slower vs. pricier but fast | Depends on the actual time gap — 20% cheaper for 20 extra minutes, sure. 20% cheaper for an extra hour, depends on cost. |
| Ship rough now vs. polish and ship later | Polish wins, unless there's a real deadline — then build something extensible, not throwaway. |
| Free/open-source (more maintenance) vs. paid (zero maintenance) | Paid is often better, but depends on how much maintenance the free option actually needs. |
| DIY to learn vs. outsource to move faster | Outsource, but I want to understand the basics — not a total black box. |
